## Mistake 1: Buying Without Checking the Encumbrance Certificate

**The Mistake**: Trusting the seller's documents without independently verifying the property's legal history through an EC.

**What Can Go Wrong**: You buy a plot from someone who has mortgaged it to a bank or NBFC. The loan was never disclosed. Three months after purchase, the bank sends you a notice because the seller defaulted — and your property is their security. Or worse: the property was already sold to someone else two years ago (double registration), and that buyer has prior legal claim.

**Real Impact**: You may lose the entire investment. Legal resolution can take 5–10 years in courts. The money spent on lawyers may equal or exceed the plot's value.

**How to Avoid**: Get a fresh Encumbrance Certificate for **minimum 30 years** from the MeeSeva portal (₹25, available in minutes). Read every entry. Ask your lawyer to review any mortgage entries, court orders, or gaps in the ownership chain.

**The Rule**: Never pay advance without a clean EC in hand.

## Mistake 2: Purchasing Plots in Unapproved Layouts

**The Mistake**: Buying a plot in a layout that has only "gram panchayat approval" — not HMDA or DTCP approval.

**What Can Go Wrong**: Panchayat approval has no legal standing for residential layout development. Banks **will not** sanction home loans for panchayat-only plots. Building permissions (GHMC/HMDA/CDMA) cannot be obtained. In worst cases, the government issues demolition notices for unauthorized constructions on such plots.

**Real Impact**: Your ₹20 lakh investment is essentially worthless for construction or resale to home builders. It may only resell to another speculative investor at a discount.

**How to Avoid**: Always verify layout approval on the **HMDA portal** (hmda.gov.in) or **DTCP portal** before buying. The approval number should be listed. Cross-check the survey number of your specific plot against the approved layout plan.

**The Rule**: No DTCP or HMDA approval = No purchase.

## Mistake 3: Paying Advance Without a Proper Agreement

**The Mistake**: Paying lakhs as advance to "block" a plot based on a verbal understanding or a flimsy letter, without a legally drafted and stamped "Agreement of Sale."

**What Can Go Wrong**: The seller backs out after receiving your advance, claims there was "no real agreement," or sells to a higher bidder. You have no legal recourse because there's no enforceable document. Or the seller later inserts additional conditions that you never agreed to.

**Real Impact**: You either lose the advance entirely or spend months in a civil dispute to recover it.

**How to Avoid**: Any advance payment must be backed by a proper **Agreement of Sale** — drafted by a lawyer, printed on stamp paper of appropriate value, and signed by both parties. The agreement should specify: property details, total price, advance paid, balance due, date of registration, consequences of default by either party.

**The Rule**: Not a single rupee without a stamped, signed agreement.

## Mistake 4: Ignoring Mutation After Registration

**The Mistake**: Registering the sale deed at the SRO and then stopping — without applying for mutation at the revenue office.

**What Can Go Wrong**: Your name appears in the SRO records (registered deed) but **not** in the revenue records (patta, 1B). The previous owner's name stays in revenue records. This creates ambiguity about who is the "legal" owner in government land records. Future disputes, inheritance issues, and resale complications multiply.

**Real Impact**: When you try to sell 5 years later, the buyer's lawyer will flag that mutation isn't done. You'll have to complete mutation then — but by that time, the previous owner may be deceased, making the process much more complicated.

**How to Avoid**: Within 60–90 days of registration, apply for mutation at the local Tahsildar/MRO office. Submit: sale deed copy, EC, and Aadhaar. Follow up diligently. Get the new patta in your name.

**The Rule**: Registration at SRO + Mutation at Revenue = Complete ownership record.

## Mistake 5: Not Verifying Whether the Seller is the Actual Owner

**The Mistake**: Assuming the person selling the property is the legal owner based on the documents they show you — without independently cross-referencing.

**What Can Go Wrong**: A Power of Attorney holder sells the property using an already-revoked or fraudulent PoA. Or someone forges a deceased owner's identity and creates false link documents. Or joint property (owned by multiple heirs) is sold by just one heir without the others' consent.

**Real Impact**: The real owner (or other co-owners) can challenge the sale in court. The court may nullify your purchase deed. You've lost the money and the property.

**How to Avoid**: Verify the seller's name matches the EC's last claimant entry. If sale is via PoA, verify the PoA is valid, recently executed, and not revoked. For inherited property, get all co-heirs' signatures and consent. Ask for the original documents — not photocopies.

**The Rule**: EC + Government ID + Link Documents must all tell the same story about ownership.

## Mistake 6: Buying Based on Location Alone Without Infrastructure Check

**The Mistake**: Falling in love with a location "near the proposed highway/metro" without verifying the basic infrastructure at and around the plot itself.

**What Can Go Wrong**: You buy a plot that's "5km from the proposed RRR interchange" — but there's no approach road to the plot. Or the area has no electricity connection, no water supply pipeline, and the nearest tar road is 2km away. The layout developer promised roads and infrastructure but disappeared after selling all plots.

**Real Impact**: Even if the broader area appreciates, your specific plot is harder to sell because buyers can't physically access or develop it without significant additional investment.

**How to Avoid**: Before finalising, physically visit the site. Check: Is there a physical paved road to the plot? Is there electricity (poles/wires)? Is there a water connection? How far is the nearest town/village with amenities? What is the approach from the main road?

**The Questions to Ask**:
- Can I drive to this plot today without a 4WD?
- Is there an existing tar/CC road from the main road?
- What is the plan for utilities? Who will execute it?

## Mistake 7: Ignoring Vastu in Residential Plots

**The Mistake**: Dismissing Vastu considerations entirely as "superstition" and buying whatever is available at the best price.

**What Can Go Wrong**: When you try to sell 5–7 years later, 70–80% of potential residential buyers in Telangana will ask about Vastu. A south-facing plot with a T-junction issue may be rejected by the majority of buyers, leaving you with a narrower market and lower bids.

**Real Impact**: Slower resale (6–18 months instead of 3–6 months) and a 10–20% lower realized price than comparable east/north-facing plots.

**How to Avoid**: Consider Vastu as a market-liquidity factor, not a personal belief. Favour east and north-facing plots. Avoid T-junctions, south-west corner plots, and plots adjacent to cremation grounds. The price premium for good-Vastu plots is worth it for exit liquidity.

## Mistake 8: Not Budgeting for Registration and Transaction Costs

**The Mistake**: Budgeting only the plot price and being surprised by the stamp duty, registration fee, and other transaction costs at the SRO.

**What Can Go Wrong**: You've arranged ₹40 lakhs for the plot and have no additional liquid funds. At registration, you discover you need ₹3 lakhs for stamp duty, ₹50,000 for registration and transfer duty, and ₹25,000 for lawyer fees. The transaction stalls because you're short on funds.

**Real Impact**: Deals collapse, sellers sell to other buyers, and you've wasted months of due diligence time.

**How to Avoid**: Always budget **an additional 8–10% on top of the plot price** for all transaction costs:
- Stamp duty: 5.5%
- Registration fee: 0.5%
- Transfer duty: 1.5%
- Lawyer fees: 0.25–0.5%
- Survey, EC, misc: 0.25%
- **Total**: ~8–9%

For a ₹40 lakh plot, keep **₹3.5–₹4 lakhs extra** for registration.

## Mistake 9: Buying in Litigation-Prone Areas Without Court Search

**The Mistake**: Buying a plot without checking whether the property or the layout is subject to any ongoing court cases.

**What Can Go Wrong**: The plot you buy is subject to a PIL (Public Interest Litigation) challenging the entire layout's approval, or there's a dispute between heirs of the original landowner that's been ongoing for 8 years in the Telangana High Court. You're now a party to this complexity.

**Real Impact**: You cannot register a sale deed if a court has passed a stay order on registration. Or you register and then discover the dispute makes your title questionable.

**How to Avoid**: 
1. Check the **eCourts portal** (ecourts.gov.in) — search by property survey number and party names for pending cases
2. Check the EC for court attachment orders
3. Ask your lawyer to do a court search at the relevant civil courts
4. Specifically ask the seller: "Are there any pending court cases related to this property?"

## Mistake 10: Not Consulting a Professional Before Buying

**The Mistake**: Trying to save the 1–2% consultant or lawyer fee and handling the transaction independently, often relying on the seller's documents and the seller's-side agent's "assurances."

**What Can Go Wrong**: Any or all of the above 9 mistakes. Without professional due diligence, you're flying blind in a complex legal and regulatory landscape where the other side (seller, developer) knows far more than you.

**Real Impact**: The cost of a single bad transaction — litigation, loss of advance, fraudulent purchase — can be **20–100x the cost of professional guidance**. People who saved ₹25,000 in consultant fees have lost ₹25 lakhs in fraudulent deals.

**How to Avoid**: Engage a qualified property lawyer for document verification. Work with a reputable real estate consultancy that has a track record and accountability. The consultant should verify documents independently — not relay the seller's version.
